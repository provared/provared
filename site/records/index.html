What the agent is for

The agent is Claude Code, a coding agent, which the author uses in their own time to read and change the code of Provared in its repository, run its tests and tools, and publish what the author approves. The permission names it, allows seven kinds of action with limits on four of them, states five rules of conduct, and was signed on 10 October 2026 at prova.red/sign/ with the author's passkey. It was renewed with permission 2, in force from 2026-10-14 19:23 UTC until 2026-12-31 23:59 UTC, with changes that its entry below shows. The author's name in it, "Pavel i", is what the author typed: a name in a record is a label, and only the keys are checked.

The permission of 14 October 2026 is signed with a different passkey from the first. The author states it is their own, made in another browser. The recorder takes a permission from a passkey it does not already trust only when told to with --new-passkey, and a checker names every passkey it trusts, as the command under "Check it yourself" does.

How it is recorded

A small program runs before and after every tool call the agent makes (Claude Code's "hooks"; the code is integrations/claude-code in the repository). Until 14 October 2026 at 19:05 UTC the hooks ran only for the common tools (reading and changing files, commands, the web and helper agents); from then, for every tool but a question to the person, a plan, a list of tasks, a skill or a search for tools, and for calls that failed as well. Before a call, the check before acting asks whether the call is within the permission; a call outside it is refused and does not run. After the call, the agent's side signs a receipt (a stub) with two keys, one of them quantum-safe, naming the kind of action, the tool, the time, and the SHA-256 fingerprint of the call's arguments. The arguments themselves, and the contents of any file, are never in the record. The kind of action is chosen by the hook from the tool's name and, for a command, from its text (a command that removes something counts as a deletion; one that pushes or publishes counts as a release), not by the agent. A recorder on the author's computer keeps the book and seals it at least once a day with three keys; the seals carry no outside time-stamp, so the time of a seal is the recorder's own word, and the checker says so.

Where it went outside its permission

The checker reports 1 entry outside the permission. Going outside is not a fault in the record: it is what a sound record shows.

  • Entry 3135, 2026-10-13T19:28:46Z, provared.code.run: [over-period-limit] With this stub there are 601 such actions in any hour. The slip allows 600.

The entry of 13 October 2026, explained: it is the 601st run of a command in one hour, where the permission allows 600. The author states that several helper agents were working at once. Each call is checked against the book as it stands when the call is asked, so calls asked at nearly the same moment near the limit are each allowed, because none of them is in the book yet, and together they can go over. The check before acting does not count calls allowed and not yet recorded; the checker reports what happened, as it should.

Check it yourself

The book is book.jsonl (6622 entries, about 45187 KB), and its permissions alone are slip.json, slip-2.json. A checker trusts nothing it is not told to trust, so name the keys: the two passkeys that signed the permissions, each by its thumbprint, and the recorder's three keys, by the fingerprint of the set. With Node.js 24.7 or later:

npm install provared
npx provared-check book.jsonl --issuer BkrSDxKyk2FW0GD0-WCyOKcRRXZs6QDLBPjlBMItPPk --issuer Wt7n9Omj4ZvioJdUV4OhP2V0pAte3HAVpI0xqL_1aIw --sealer OOgdiubfBSeXkjMOi5bUcbFyXpuuorxC2gdtV2oyAhw

Or in a browser, on the checking page: choose the file, and paste the thumbprints, one to a line, into the passkey box and the fingerprint into the recorder box. No browser tried on the author's computer on 13 October 2026 (Chrome 155, Edge 155 and Brave 155) has the third method a seal is signed with (SLH-DSA), so in a browser the page says that the check was not complete and gives no pass; all three check the receipts' quantum-safe signatures (ML-DSA). The command-line checker checks everything.

What this record does not show

The checker says this itself, at the end of its answer:

  • It shows what was recorded, not what was left out. An agent that acts and writes no stub leaves no trace here.
  • A one-sided stub shows what the agent's side said, not what the other side did.
  • A refusal shows what the service's side said, not what the agent did.
  • It does not show that an action was wise, lawful or wanted.
  • A rule of conduct in a slip, such as "never claim to be a human being", is the person's signed instruction. No check can show that the agent kept it.
  • It does not show who was holding the device when the passkey signed.
  • A name in a record is a label. Only keys are checked.
  • Nothing here is time-stamped by an outside service that you named as trusted. So it does not show when the record was made, and the latest stubs under a slip or a pass, or a slip or a pass with all its stubs, could have been removed.

And two things of this record's own. A refused call leaves no entry, so the record does not show how often the agent was stopped. Every receipt is one-sided, because no other party takes part in a tool call, so each shows what the agent's side said.

One gap in this record, stated: on 11 October 2026, seven tool calls of a helper agent were taken and not recorded. The recorder could not replace the book file while another program was reading it, and the hook reported each as "taken and not in the record". The fault and its fix are in the change log; the seven calls stay unrecorded.
