Provared

Provared in plain words

Three minutes, for a reader who does not write code. Evidence, not a verdict.

Provared proves what an AI agent was allowed to do, and what it then did. Its checkpoint can also stop, before it happens, an action that the person did not allow. It is for businesses that use AI agents, and for whoever has to check them.

Companies are starting to let AI agents act for them: order, pay, send, change records. When one goes wrong, nobody can prove what it was allowed to do. That is the gap Provared fills: a flight recorder for AI agents.

The problem

An AI agent does not just answer. It acts. It books, orders, pays, sends and changes records. A person sets it going and looks away. That is the point of it, and it is also the problem.

When something goes wrong, the question is simple: who allowed that? Today the answer is a log file, written by the same software that is being questioned, which anyone could have edited.

Suppose, in an invented example, a charity tells an agent: reorder office supplies, up to £500 a month. In June the agent pays a supplier £4,800. In December the auditor asks: who allowed that?

The rules are arriving. From 2 December 2027, Europe's AI Act requires a high-risk AI system to record automatically what it does (Article 12), and whoever deploys one to keep those logs for at least six months (Article 26).

What Provared is

Four parts, each with a short name.

  • Slip, the permissionA person signs it on their phone, with a fingerprint or face: what the agent may do, how much, with whom, until when. Like a signed school-trip form.
  • Stub, the receiptOne for every action, signed by the agent and by whoever it dealt with. Like a ticket stub.
  • Seal, the sealed bookThe receipts chained in order and dated by an outside clock, so that none can be changed, moved or taken out of the middle unnoticed. Like a wax-sealed ledger.
  • Show, chosen pages with proofThe inspector sees the pages they need, with proof that they belong to the book, and the rest stays closed. Like a certified extract.

And a free checker, which anyone can run on their own device. It answers one question in seconds: did the agent stay within what it was allowed? Yes, or no and where.

It can also stop an action before it happens. Provared's checkpoint stands between an agent and the systems it uses, and holds the systems' passwords and keys in place of the agent. It blocks any action the permission does not allow before the action reaches the system, and writes a signed receipt for each action it lets through. The passwords are out of the agent's reach only where it cannot read the checkpoint's files, for example when the checkpoint runs under another user account; and the agent must have no other way into the systems. The checkpoint comes in two versions. The guard is for the add-on tools that AI assistants use to act; a company can run it on its own server and add it to the claude.ai assistant, and the owner confirms each new connection with a fingerprint or face. The gateway is for business systems the AI reaches with no add-on tool, such as finance or HR.

How it works

The drawing below follows the sample record that ships with the software: an invented office agent, allowed up to £200 of supplies, which goes over that limit, without the approval it needed, at its fourth order.

Today, the evidence is scattered across several systems, written by the software being questioned, easy to change, and nothing links the payment to what was agreed. It is the company's word against the doubt.

With Provared, the auditor is handed the permission the manager signed, the receipt the supplier countersigned, and the sealed, dated book they sit in. Anyone can check it in seconds.

In a large company

When a company lets AI agents act in its systems, three questions matter: what is each agent allowed to do, what stops it doing more, and how can both be proved later? The drawing answers them from the top down: the company's people, its AI, Provared's checkpoint, the company's systems, the book of receipts that is kept, and the proof handed to an auditor.

Two words used below. An action is one thing the AI asks a company system to do, such as raising an order, changing a record or sending an email. The checkpoint is Provared's software that stands between the AI and the company's systems and checks each action. It comes in two versions, the guard and the gateway.

For the checkpoint to work, the AI must reach the company's systems only through it. IT gives the systems' passwords to the checkpoint, not to the AI; runs the checkpoint where the AI cannot read its files; and blocks any other route to the systems. IT also writes the rules that tell the checkpoint what each request to a system means, such as "an order of £8,000": the check is only as right as those rules. A cancellation the manager signs does not yet reach the checkpoint by itself; IT has to carry it out by hand, for example by stopping the agent's access. Agents the company builds itself can also have the check built in, with Provared's ready-made code, but there it works only if the agent's own software asks for it.

One task, in an invented example:

  1. The finance director signs, on their phone, what a purchasing agent may do: raise purchase orders, up to £10,000 in total and at most ten orders, using only the finance system and the document search, never deleting anything, until the end of the quarter.
  2. IT gives Provared's checkpoint the passwords of the finance system and the document search, takes them away from the agent, runs the checkpoint where the agent cannot read its files, and blocks any other route to the two systems. It also writes the rules that tell the checkpoint what each request means.
  3. The agent looks up the purchasing policy in the document search and raises an order for £8,000. Each action is passed on with a signed receipt. It then asks to raise an order for £14,000, over the limit. The checkpoint blocks it before the finance system sees it, and signs a note of the block.
  4. Each checkpoint puts its receipts into a book, in order, and seals it at the times IT has set. Where an outside time service is set up, it dates each seal.
  5. Months later an auditor asks who allowed the £8,000 order. The company hands over two pages of the book: the permission and that order's receipt. The auditor's checker, on the auditor's own computer, shows the signed permission and whether that order, on its own, went outside it. Whether the £10,000 total was kept, and the permission not cancelled first, needs the whole book. Evidence, not a verdict.

Where each part stands today:

  • Published, and free for anyone to use: the signing page, the checkpoint in both versions, Provared's ready-made code for agents, and the checker.
  • The application, where a manager is to read each agent's book and sign a renewal or a cancellation, is not yet open to others, and cannot yet pass either back to the checkpoint.
  • Sealing by a separate team works with the guard only, on the guard's own computer, and is not yet a published package. The gateway seals with keys of its own for now.
  • The checking page in a web browser cannot yet check a sealed book in full; the checker installed on a computer can.
  • As far as the author knows, no company has yet run all of these together. The section "Where it is today", below, says how far each part has been tried.

Why it is different

  1. Several large platforms for AI agents now offer to hold the passwords of the systems an agent uses, and to check each action. Provared's checkpoint does that too, and adds the proof: what you allowed, in a permission you signed on your phone, and what the agent did through it, in a signed receipt for each action. Anyone can check both later, on their own computer, rather than rely on a platform's own logs.
  2. Other projects record what an agent did. Provared also records what it was allowed to do, and compares the two automatically.
  3. The permission has limits a machine can check: £200 in total, five orders, this supplier only, until Friday, never delete.
  4. The person can cancel at any time. The agent signs that it was told. The book shows if it carried on.
  5. A permission can be handed on to a helper agent, but never widened.
  6. The signatures are designed to stay valid for decades, even against quantum computers.
  7. It is free and open, built on published standards. The core is already a draft at the Internet Engineering Task Force (IETF), the body that sets the internet's standards.

At least four other open projects make signed, time-stamped receipts. Provared adds the permission the person signed, with limits a machine can check, and a checker that says whether each action stayed within it.

The whole of Provared

Detail for technical readers: this section and "Where it is today". "Hard questions" and "What a record does not prove", after them, are for everyone.

Every piece writes or reads the same kind of record, and the same free checker reads them all. In short: a person signs a permission at the signing page; the guard, or another recorder beside the agent, checks the agent's actions against it and writes a receipt for each; the sealer seals the book; an inspector checks it with the checker.

The format

  • The format description, open for anyone to use, says exactly what each part holds and how it is checked. Its core is an Internet-Draft at the IETF. Shared test files, and a kit that compares another program's answers with them, let anyone build their own version and show that it agrees.

For the person who gives the permission

  • The signing page, sign.prova.red. A person signs a permission, a renewal or a cancellation there with a passkey. That address serves nothing else.

Beside the agent: ways to record it

  • The guard, provared-mcp-executor 0.5.3, for agents that use MCP tool servers. It stands in front of the tools, refuses any call the permission does not allow, and signs a receipt for each call it lets through. It runs on the agent's computer, or over the network for several agents, each with its own permission and record, and can be added to claude.ai as a custom connector. It can also check what a call asks for, for example which file names a file tool is given, seal the receipts into a book, and, where a time-stamp service is set up, have each seal dated by it.
  • The library, provared 0.9.2, in JavaScript and Python, for an agent a developer writes: each action is checked against the permission first and receipted after.
  • The recorder beside Claude Code, provared-claude-code 0.2.0. Claude Code is an AI coding agent. The recorder checks Claude Code's tool calls against the permission before they run, and receipts each one taken. It leaves out eight tools that only ask the person something or change what the agent itself holds, such as its plan or its list of tasks. It keeps the author's own record, which is public.
  • Add-ons for LangChain and Pydantic AI, 0.2.0 each, two of the most-used toolkits for building agents.
  • A tool server for one Shopify store, provared-shopify 0.1.0, made to run behind the guard. Through it an agent can read products and orders, change a price, make and stop discount codes, and refund an order; the guard checks each call against the permission first, and the server itself checks none. It has been run only against a made-up Shopify, in its tests, not against a real store.
  • The gateway, for agents that act through web services: it stands in front of each service, holds its credential, refuses any call the permission does not allow, and receipts each call. It can also countersign each call it lets through, under keys of its own; that is the gateway's own word, not the service's. provared-gateway 0.1.1 on npm; see its folder in the repository.

Keeping the book

  • The sealer, a small program run by someone other than whoever runs the agent. It holds the keys that seal the book, so they are not on the agent's computer, and, as long as its own state is kept, it never seals two different versions of one book. Of each book it sees the permission the book starts with, and of every later line only its fingerprint, not what it says. In the repository, not yet a published package.

Checking

  • The checker, free, on the command line or as the checking page in a browser, which also works as one file with no connection. It asks nobody. The browsers tried so far lack one of the three signature methods a seal uses, so in a browser a sealed book never passes; the command-line checker checks it in full.

Seeing and controlling the agents

  • The Provared application, a web application where a person reads each agent's book and, from it, renews or cancels a permission at the signing page. provared-connect 0.2.0 links the recorder beside an agent to it with one line copied from the book's page. Each line of the book reaches the application's server encrypted with the book's key, which the server is not given, so the server cannot read it. Not yet open to anyone but the author.

Where it is today

The library, the guard, the gateway, the recorder beside Claude Code, the Shopify tool server and the two add-ons are published, for anyone to install, on npm and on the Python Package Index. The core of the format is an Internet-Draft at the IETF; an Internet-Draft is a working document, not a standard. Every line of code and every test is public on GitHub, and the checking page, which also works as one file with no connection, has a sample record to try.

The library depends on no outside code in JavaScript, and on one package in Python. It sends nothing home, holds no money and starts no payment. It is held to more than 750 automated tests, and to more than 14,000 shared test cases that the JavaScript and Python versions must answer alike. It has been through more than 80 rounds of review by AI reviewers, each a fresh session given the code, the documents and a brief of what to look at. Until 7 November 2026 each reviewer likely also had the author's private planning notes loaded, so could see the author's reasoning; since then reviewers start without them. Every fault was fixed, or stated as a limit, and kept as a test. No person other than the author has reviewed it yet. That is an invitation.

The guard works with tool servers that speak the Model Context Protocol (MCP), the common way for AI agents to reach tools. It is published on npm as provared-mcp-executor, version 0.5.3, and is listed in the official MCP Registry. Version 0.4.2, published on 15 November 2026, fixed two weaknesses in every earlier version: in front of some tool servers, an agent could have a tool run that the guard never checked, with no receipt or with a receipt for a different tool. Its security advisory says which tool servers are affected. Version 0.5.2, published on 23 November 2026, fixed a further weakness in versions 0.3.0 to 0.5.1: over the network, with several agents, one agent could list, read or cancel another agent's unfinished jobs ("tasks") at a tool server that keeps them for whoever holds its credential. That advisory has the details. npm marks every version before 0.5.2 as out of date ("deprecated"). A demonstration runs the guard in front of a made-up tool server, with no passkey and no settings; its records prove nothing about anyone. Over the network, on 15 November 2026, an earlier build of version 0.5.0 was tried with claude.ai and a server on the internet: claude.ai connected after one passkey prompt, and the guard let two tool calls through and refused a third. Apart from that, it has been tried only with every program on one computer, not yet on separate computers.

It is one author, in their own time, on their own equipment. Building started on 3 October 2026, with AI tools.

Hard questions

A log file does this already.A log is written by the one being questioned, and can be edited. This is signed by the person, the agent and the other side, and dated by an outside clock. An auditor would rather be handed the second.

One person, in spare time?Yes. That is why it is open, held to 750 tests, reviewed in more than 80 rounds by AI reviewers and not yet by anyone else, and published where anyone can check it. If the author vanishes, the records still check. The standard does not depend on one person.

How long did it take?Building started on 3 October 2026. Published on 6 October. Built with AI tools, reviewed in more than 80 rounds by AI reviewers, every fault fixed or stated as a limit, and kept as a test. Judge the code, not the calendar.

Can it stop a bad agent?The guard can, for the tools it stands in front of, and the gateway for the web services it stands in front of: each refuses any call outside the slip before the call reaches the tool or service, and the agent cannot go round it as long as the guard or gateway alone holds the credentials and the agent has no other way to the tools or services. Without the guard or gateway, the check stops only an agent whose own software asks it. Neither can judge right from wrong. Evidence, not a verdict.

Does it hold my money or my data?It holds no money, starts no payment, issues no token, and sends nothing home. The guard and the gateway keep your tools' and services' credentials on the computer where you run them, and, where set up, their own copy of each call. The application, when it opens to others, keeps each book on its server encrypted with the book's key, which the server is not given, so it cannot read the book. The checker asks nobody.

Why bother with quantum computers?A record must still check in ten years. Today's signatures may not. It costs the user nothing.

Prove it.The pages linked above are public. Or give a technical person thirty minutes to try to break it; a hole found is fixed in public. Found one? Open an issue on GitHub. It is fixed in public, and you are named in the change log if you wish.

What a record does not prove

  • What was left out. An agent that acts and writes no receipt leaves no trace.
  • Who was holding the phone when the permission was signed.
  • Whether an action was wise, lawful or wanted.
  • That a name is right. A name in a record is a label; only the keys are checked.

Dictionary

AI agent: software that does not just answer but acts: it books, orders, pays, sends and changes records.

Passkey: the sign-in built into a phone or computer, unlocked with a fingerprint or face. A person signs a permission with it.

Key: a secret that makes signatures, held by one person, agent or service. Its public half lets anyone check them.

Signature: a mark that only the holder of a key can make, and that anyone with the public half can check. Each record is signed by whoever wrote it.

Countersignature: the other side's signature on a receipt, saying that the action happened.

Fingerprint, or hash: a short code worked out from a file. Change one letter of the file and the code changes, so it shows whether a copy is exact.

Chain: each receipt carries the fingerprint of the one before it, so none can be removed or reordered unnoticed.

Time-stamp, the outside clock: a signed statement from an independent service that a fingerprint existed by a given time. It shows that a record is no newer than that time, not when it was made.

Recorder: whoever keeps the sealed book.

Credential: a password, key or token that lets software use a tool or a service.

Guard: software that stands between an agent and its tools, holds the tools' credentials in place of the agent (it keeps them from the agent only where the agent cannot read the guard's files), and refuses any call the permission does not allow. Provared's guard is the package provared-mcp-executor.

Sealer: a program, run apart from the agent, that holds the keys that seal a book.

Gateway: the guard's counterpart for web services: it stands in front of each service an agent uses, holds its credential, refuses any call the permission does not allow, and receipts each call.

Checkpoint: the guard and the gateway together: Provared's software between an AI agent and a company's systems, which checks each action against the permission.

Tool server: a program an agent calls to do one kind of thing, such as reading files, searching documents or changing a shop's prices.

RAG, retrieval-augmented generation: a way of making an AI model look things up in a store of the company's own documents before it answers. Where the search is offered to the agent as a tool, Provared sees it as one more tool call: it can allow or refuse it, and receipts it, but it does not judge what comes back. Documents offered as MCP resources, and searches that do not pass the guard, are not checked.

MCP, the Model Context Protocol: the common way for AI agents to reach tools, such as reading files or searching the web.

Checker: the free program that reads a record and says whether it is intact, and whether the agent stayed within its permission.

Quantum-safe signature: a signature method that a future quantum computer is not expected to break. Provared uses two methods side by side, so that a record still checks in decades.

Open source: code published for anyone to read, use and build on. Provared's code is under the Apache 2.0 licence and its documents under Creative Commons Attribution.

IETF: the Internet Engineering Task Force, the body that sets the internet's standards.

Internet-Draft: a working document submitted to the IETF. Not a standard.

npm, Python Package Index: the two main places where developers install ready-made software from.

LangChain, Pydantic AI: two of the most-used toolkits for building AI agents.

Evidence, not a verdict: a record shows what was signed and by which keys. People decide what it means.
